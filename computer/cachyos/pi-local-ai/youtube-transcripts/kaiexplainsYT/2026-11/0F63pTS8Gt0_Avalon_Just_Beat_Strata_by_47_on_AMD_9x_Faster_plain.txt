Where do the other 28 tokens per second come from? The answer is speculative drafting, which on a mixture of experts model is much harder to do efficiently than on a standard dense model. The concept is that a small draft head guesses the next couple of tokens before the main model confirms them and then we check all the guesses in one pass. So, if the guesses were right, we got multiple tokens for roughly the cost of one, which makes it a bit like auto complete for inference. On a dense transformer, this is fairly clean because when we check three guessed tokens, each weight gets read once and reused across all three checks. So, the verification costs only slightly more than writing a single token. But on a mixture of experts, this falls apart. Every guessed token picks its own 10 experts per layer independently. So, checking three guesses might touch up to three times as many distinct experts as a single token would and the memory bill explodes. Avalon's draft head agreed with the main model on 80.7% of positions on a fixed test.
